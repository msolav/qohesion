# Changelog

All notable changes are listed here, newest first. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/). Versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html). While the major number is 0 the project is in alpha: any minor version may change behaviour or data formats, and each entry says so under "Upgrade notes".

## [Unreleased]

No version has been published yet. `0.1.0` is the first planned tag.

### Added
- Several questions per poll (ADR 0010): a poll holds up to 20 single-choice or yes-no questions, 100 options in all. The builder adds, orders and removes questions after the first (with their texts in every language); the ballot has a page for each question, a summary with a Change button for each answer, and the check when the vote cannot be changed; the one-page form shows every question. One ballot holds every answer, with one receipt, sealed as one block. Results, the named answers and the published list are given for each question. A threshold closing rule is refused with several questions. Sealed ballots of earlier polls open as before.
- Review of ADR 0008 and 0009 work by six independent reviewers with two skeptics per finding (13 confirmed). Fixed: sealed ballots are padded to one length, so their size no longer shows the choice; the published ledger and ballot list are in the order of the receipt codes, not of casting, and the Merkle root is a function of the set of ballots; a request keeps the administrators who were eligible and the rule in force when it was made, so a key made later cannot answer it and a change of rule does not change it; closing by hand, cancelling, adding participants to an open poll, creating and revoking keys, deleting the organization and opening a scheduled poll early all need a permit from an approved request once approvals apply; a scheduled poll's roll is fixed; unscheduling clears the approval and the kept name; links are held before sending so that a second press or the timer cannot write to the same people, and a failed automatic reminder run is tried again; approval answers move focus to a status line and have distinct names for assistive technology.
- Deliverability warnings (issue #91): the roll marks participants at providers that filed our test messages as junk (Outlook.com, Hotmail, Yahoo, AOL, GMX, iCloud and others) and tells the administrator to ask them to check junk or to send their link another way; the invitation asks recipients to mark it as not junk if it landed there.
- Test edition, ADR 0009 (approvals between administrators): the organization chooses a rule (none, majority or all), what an abstention counts as, and how many hours a request stays open. Once a rule is on, changing it is itself a request. With the rule on and two or more administrators, publishing a poll waits for the other administrators, who approve, reject or abstain on an Approvals page with a badge in the navigation. Approval covers the exact content (settings, texts, options, dates, participants): any change to the draft voids the answers and says what changed. A change of the organization's name, language or time zone goes through approval too. The ballot's rules page states how the poll was approved, with a content code. Not yet: approval for keys, cancelling, closing early and late additions; the setup confirmations; the tamper-evident record (issues #83, #85, #86).
- ADR 0009 (proposed): organization governance and approvals, with issues #81 to #90. Implemented first: the organization's name is fixed while a poll is scheduled or open, each poll keeps the name it was published under (ballot, emails, calendar file, results), and the poll history lists the latest entry first.
- Roll: a poll states whether participants can be added after it opens (a setting of the poll, off in the Secret ballot and Maximum transparency presets), and the ballot tells voters. The roll shows how many reminders each participant got and the day of the last one. Addresses are kept as typed (a Gmail address keeps its dots) while the same person is still counted once.
- Fix: links sent before the exact time was recorded were offered again as first links instead of reminders. The page for a link already used says what the receipt code is for.
- Test edition, ADR 0008 (stage C): a paged voting flow. A poll shows an introduction, then its rules with an acknowledgement the participant must tick, then the question, then, when the vote cannot be changed, a check page that states so before the ballot is sent. The receipt shows the closing time and a calendar file (`/v/<link>/calendar.ics`) with an alarm 24 hours before; the rules page links the same file. The file names the poll and the organization and holds no personal link. Without JavaScript, or by choice of the administrator (a setting of the poll), the ballot is one page. Fixes "1 participants".
- Test edition, ADR 0008 (stages A and B): presets (Secret ballot, Maximum transparency, Open vote); publishable verifiable count (ballot list with receipt and choice, the counting key of a sealed poll, ledger download); cancelling is recorded (who, when, how many ballots) and a poll can forbid cancelling once voting began; the counting key can be cut in shares, one per administrator, with a set number needed to open the ballots (Shamir over GF(256)). The receipt page no longer claims that votes cannot be changed after submission.
- Question and form engine (`packages/engine`): single choice, multiple choice, text, number, colour (with contrast rules) and toggle questions; conditional steps; validation with error codes; list of untranslated texts.
- Version tools in the engine: parsing and comparing versions, release channel (alpha, beta, stable), display text, and an assessment of an update from one version to another.
- Email package (`packages/email`): Brevo and Resend transports behind one interface, failover for service emails (not for the voting lane), a daily budget guard with a reserve for service emails, message checks (addresses, header injection) and one-click unsubscribe headers. A script (`scripts/send-test-email.mjs`) sends one test message to check the sending domain. Templates for the verification email and the administrator sign-in email, in French and English, with no image or tracking in the HTML. A second script (`scripts/inspect-eml.py`) reads a received message and fails on a failed DKIM or DMARC check, a tracking image, a provider footer or a wrapped link.
- Roll package (`packages/roll`): address checks and canonical form, typo suggestions for common domains, a check that a domain accepts mail (MX, null MX, implicit MX), keyed fingerprints and overlap measures, the status machine of an address across the registration, frozen, voting and closed phases, the frozen roll as a count and a short code, amendments with approvals, administrator setup checks (threshold keys) and the assurance level with what is missing for the next one.
- Ledger package (`packages/ledger`): the append-only ledger of a vote as a Merkle tree (RFC 9162, SHA-256) with inclusion and consistency proofs, receipt codes, and a fixed text form for checkpoints. Roots match the Certificate Transparency reference roots. The encryption and tally scheme is not part of it.
- Web application skeleton (`apps/web`, SvelteKit with the Node adapter): French and English display chosen from the address, a remembered choice or the browser setting (French when the browser language is neither); the ballot screen built from the engine, working with and without JavaScript, in the two templates; `/version` endpoint; a changelog page that renders this file; version and source-code link in every footer; Content Security Policy and protective headers in builds (the referrer policy is `same-origin`, so that browsers send a valid `Origin` with form posts). No ballot is recorded yet.
- Docker image and Compose file for the web application (read-only file system, unprivileged user, no capabilities), an install guide (`docs/install.md`), and a CI job that builds the image and checks `/version`. The footer's source link can be pointed to a modified copy with `QOHESION_SOURCE_URL` (AGPL).
- Ballot-screen prototype with two templates (Seed Packet, Ballot Paper), an organization colour scheme checked for contrast, and French and English display.
- Placeholder website at qohesion.app and a development preview at dev.qohesion.app.
- Website (`design/site`): the pages of a website in French (at the root) and English (under `/en/`), with a navy look taken from the first landing page. The home page runs the real ballot screen prototype in two phone-sized frames, shows the three planned modes, an example receipt code from the ledger tests and the hardening settings of `compose.yml`, and links to the pricing, terms of use, name and logo, and the English documentation. Links are checked and a broken one stops the build; what the home page claims about the ledger and the container is tested against the repository.
- Ballot screen: the chosen option shows its meaning, green with a check for In favour, red with a cross for Against, grey with a dash for Abstention, in both templates (prototype and application).
- Website home page: a carousel of ballot screen templates, a five-step diagram of why a vote stays secret and checkable, modes side by side, and a price ladder on the pricing page. The copy describes the offer in the present tense.
- Password-gated development preview: `design/deploy-preview.sh` publishes the website to `dev.qohesion.app` as a Cloudflare Worker (`design/site/gate`) that asks for a password before serving anything and refuses everyone when no password is set. The password is a Worker secret and is not in the repository.
- Test edition (ADR 0007, `QOHESION_EDITION=test`): create an organization, sign in with a key, create polls with one question (in favour, against, abstention, or one choice among several), keep a roll with a personal voting link per address, open and close polls, vote with a receipt code, and read the results. Results settings are fixed when a poll opens: shown live or when the poll closes, to the administrators, to the members on the roll, or to anyone with the address. Live results update every few seconds. Closed polls publish their ledger and check a receipt with an inclusion proof. Keys carry the owner role, the administrator role, or both; an owner manages keys and can delete the organization. Ballots are stored without encryption in a local SQLite file, no email is sent, and every screen says so. `scripts/smoke-test-edition.mjs` walks the whole flow against a running server and runs in CI; `compose.test-edition.yml` adds the data volume.
- Three more ballot templates, in the application, the prototype and the website carousel: Boarding Pass (a ticket with a strip and a receipt motif), Guide Map (a cream ground with a disc and full-width coloured pills) and Standard (the conventional card). Organizations choose any of the five per poll and as their default. All keep the per-option marks and meet WCAG AA contrast.
- Optional sealed ballots in the test edition: a poll can encrypt each ballot on arrival and be counted with a counting key that is shown once and kept nowhere; the ballot screen states whether ballots are encrypted. An optional password (`QOHESION_GATE_PASSWORD`) can sit in front of a whole test server.
- Email in the test edition: with `QOHESION_BREVO_API_KEY` set, an administrator sends each member who has not voted their personal link from the roll page, after confirming permission to contact them. The message states whether ballots are encrypted and who sees the results, carries no image or tracking, and is sent through the daily budget guard (300 a day, 50 kept for service emails). The roll shows the day each link was sent. `scripts/start-test-edition.sh` reads the key and the password from private files in the home folder.
- Switching language no longer reloads the page or erases what was typed in a form: the switcher navigates in place, the document language follows, and typed text is kept in the form state.
- Poll builder in the test edition: a guided flow of five steps with a live preview of the real ballot in the chosen template, keyboard-first option editing (Enter adds, Backspace removes, Alt with the arrows moves, drag and buttons too), and an icon and a colour for each option as advanced parameters (16 icons, 7 colours, contrast checked). Icons and colours show on the ballot, in the preview and in the results. The ballot is now one component for real ballots and the preview.
- Test edition: one organization per installation (the first visit sets it up, later visits sign in); dates for every poll (an opening date that schedules it, a required closing date, typed in the organization's time zone); closing rules (after a number of votes, at a share of the roll, or when an option has more than half of the roll or a chosen share); an option to let members change their vote on sealed polls (their last ballot counts); a single setting for how option colours are drawn; icons on the setting cards; and the blue Qohesion look as the default template. The sealed ballot's ledger keeps every ballot.
- Console look: titles and figures in Outfit, the roll shown as a row of dots (one for each member, filled when they have voted) on the poll list and the overview, the roll code on a notched stub, perforated section rules, a drawn empty state, and a majority mark on result bars. The replacement of the four equal figure tiles by one turnout line follows the audit of the console.
- Cancelling a poll erases its ballots so that its results are never revealed; polls are either fully anonymous or show each answer with its address (partly hidden for the public), and the ballot says which; actions ask in the console's own dialog instead of the browser's.
- Poll content in several languages: administrators choose which languages a poll is offered in (French, English; one is the default), write the question, the details and every option in each, and see the ballot preview in the language being edited. Members read the poll in the language they chose, or in the default one when theirs is not offered. A poll with an offered language that lacks the question or an option is refused. The top bar's controls share one height and outline, and the poll's turnout and roll code form one ticket.
- Poll title and question: a poll has a title (its name in lists and emails) and a question (the line participants answer), each translated like the rest. Reminders: a participant is written to at most once a day, an administrator sends "Send to participants" and "Send a reminder", and a poll can send reminders by itself, every day at 9 a.m. or once a day before it closes, after the administrator confirms permission. The ballot shows icons for anonymous or named and encrypted or not. "Members" became "participants" in the interface. Interface texts were rewritten in a plainer register. ADR 0008 proposes presets, a publishable count, a paged voting flow, several questions and calendar reminders.
- Pricing: the "Event pass" is now the "Poll pass", since a poll is already the product's word for a vote.
- Offer comparison table on the pricing page: rules between rows only, a larger price row, the paid offer's column tinted.
- Licensing framework (AGPL-3.0-or-later for code, CC BY-SA 4.0 for documentation, CC BY 4.0 for templates, name and logo not open), checked with the REUSE tool.
- Decision records 0001 to 0005 (accepted for the alpha on 2026-11-26, open to revision) and 0006 (pricing, proposed), a draft name and logo policy, a brief for legal review, glossary, content style guide, draft terms of use for the hosted service.
